What the registry means

A precise floor,
not a journal ceiling.

Palomar gives a stable, searchable home to formal results that clear a published minimum standard. It separates machine verification, mathematical description, and presentation so each can be inspected.

From snapshot to record

  1. 01

    Immutable source

    A GitHub issue points to a public repository and full Git commit. Moving branches are never registry evidence.

  2. 02

    Small statement surface

    Challenge.lean states the advertised result. Its transitive imports may come only from Lean core, Mathlib, Tau Ceti, or an already indexed Palomar project.

  3. 03

    Sandboxed comparison

    Comparator rebuilds Challenge and Solution separately under landrun, checks exact declaration identity and permitted axioms, then replays the proof through the Lean kernel.

  4. 04

    Editorial review

    Versioned AI review checks metadata, informal-to-Lean alignment, definitions, prior literature, notability, and clarity. Failures, uncertainty, and permanent warnings stay visible.

  5. 05

    Append-only publication

    An accepted result becomes an immutable JSON record with a permanent identifier and version. Corrections add versions; history is not rewritten.

Palomar does say

The pinned Solution proves the pinned Challenge.

It also says that a recorded editorial process found the statement and metadata responsible enough to index under the policy version shown.

Palomar does not say

This is peer review, novelty certification, or endorsement.

Readers still need mathematical judgment. The registry makes the exact object, checks, trust boundary, and warnings easier to locate and audit.
