About Palomar

What the registry means

Palomar gives a stable, searchable home to formal results that clear a published minimum standard. It separates machine verification, mathematical description, and presentation so each can be inspected.

From snapshot to record

  1. 1.

    Fixed source files

    Each submission points to an exact version of a public repository, so the files that Palomar checked cannot later change.

  2. 2.

    Readable statement file

    Challenge.lean states the advertised result. The definitions it uses may come only from Lean core, Mathlib, Tau Ceti, or another project already listed by Palomar.

  3. 3.

    Independent proof check

    Lean and Comparator mechanically check that the recorded Solution proves the recorded formal Challenge under the listed axiom and dependency rules.

  4. 4.

    Documented review

    A separate AI-mediated editorial review judges whether the formal Challenge matches the written mathematical claim and examines its definitions, prior literature, significance, and clarity. It is not human peer review or a novelty certificate; problems and uncertainties remain visible.

  5. 5.

    Permanent, append-only record

    An accepted result becomes an immutable JSON record identified by both its Palomar ID and version. Later versions may change the theorem, source, authors, or subject radically. Cite an explicit version for stable meaning; an ID without a version means the latest record and may change meaning.

Palomar does say

Mechanical proof checking and editorial judgment are separate.

Lean and Comparator check the exact formal proof claim. The documented AI-mediated review separately judges the link from that formal statement to the informal mathematical claim under the policy version shown.

Palomar does not say

This is peer review, novelty certification, or endorsement.

Readers still need mathematical judgment. The registry makes the exact statement, proof checks, dependencies, and warnings easier to find and inspect.
